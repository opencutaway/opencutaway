# Inspiration (do not copy)

**This document owns** which books inform tone and world, and the do-not-copy line for each.
**It does not own** lesson text, art files, or the object catalog.

Open Cutaway is inspired by books about reading the built world and about everyday work. Inspiration is not a license to copy.

## Cutaway systems

- David Macaulay, *Underground* — a cutaway street versus stacked systems in the soil.
- Brian Hayes, *Infrastructure: A Guide to the Industrial Landscape* — the everyday industrial landscape is readable and significant.

Take: cross-sections, named parts, the sense that ordinary streets are thick with hidden paths.

## Everyday work, overlapping and specific

- Richard Scarry, *What Do People Do All Day?* — a busy, readable cross-section of jobs, vehicles, shops, and civic work happening at once.

Take: occupational density; layered town activity; “people doing jobs” clarity; warm and specific rather than an abstract systems diagram.

Do not take: characters, character names, Busytown, animal-people casts, book layouts, or any copyrighted sentence or drawing.

## Do not copy

Do not copy text, photos, drawings, chapter lists, character names, town names, or taxonomy from any of these books. Do not scrape them. Do not add their files to this repository. Do not paraphrase a chapter into a “lesson.”

Write original kid-facing copy. Draw original cutaways, object portraits, and chain-strips. For photographs in v0, use only US public domain or CC0 sources with a row in `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Visual language (later content)

Learn-mode graphics should mix original cutaways/cross-sections, object portraits, and chain-strips. Scenes should feel busy and legible: many jobs in one view, overlapping activity, readable at a glance. This is not a node-link graph explorer. No cute anthropomorphic infrastructure.
